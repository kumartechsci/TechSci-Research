<p><strong>Role of Enzymes in Consumer Goods</strong></p>
<p>Enzymes serve as substitutes for preservatives, stabilizers, and chemical additives across industries. In food processing, they break down complex nutrients, making food more digestible and improving texture, flavor, and shelf life. In beverages, they enhance clarity and stability. These benefits resonate with health-conscious consumers who seek better nutritional profiles and overall quality.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>Apart from food applications, enzymes are widely used in textiles to reduce harsh chemical treatments, improving both sustainability and cost efficiency. In the pharmaceutical sector, they aid in drug formulation, biofuel production, and various bioprocesses. Because enzyme production relies on controlled biotechnological processes, it is considered eco-friendly compared to chemical manufacturing methods.</p>

<p><strong>Micro-organisms as the Leading Source</strong></p>
<p>Micro-organisms accounted for the largest share of India&rsquo;s industrial enzymes market in 2024. They are projected to continue dominating throughout the forecast period. Bacteria and fungi produce a wide range of enzymes suitable for multiple industrial uses, from agriculture to pharmaceuticals.</p>
<p>The main advantages of microbial enzymes include scalability, consistency, and adaptability. Micro-organisms can be cultured in controlled environments to produce enzymes on a large scale. Their ability to be genetically engineered allows manufacturers to develop custom enzymes suited for specific industrial processes.</p>
<p>Microbial enzymes are also cost-effective because they provide high yields and can act on a broad range of organic compounds. This makes them attractive for industries dealing with complex raw materials, such as food processing and biofuel production.</p>
