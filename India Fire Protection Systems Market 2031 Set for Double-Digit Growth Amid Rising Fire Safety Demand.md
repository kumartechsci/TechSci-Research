<p style="text-align: justify;"><strong>Cost Constraints and SME Hesitation Pose Challenges</strong></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">Despite the evident need, one of the key barriers to widespread adoption&mdash;especially among small and medium enterprises (SMEs)&mdash;remains the cost of installation and maintenance of fire protection systems. Automated fire protection systems such as sprinklers, detectors, and integrated suppression technologies involve considerable capital expenditure. This cost factor deters many small businesses and residential building owners from investing in full-fledged fire safety systems.</p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">As a result, many SMEs and startups still rely on low-cost alternatives such as handheld fire extinguishers or manual suppression systems, which do not offer the same level of protection or response efficiency as automated systems.</p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">Moreover, retrofitting fire protection systems in existing buildings adds another layer of complexity and expense. High-rise and older buildings often require significant structural adjustments, which increase costs and may interrupt regular business operations during installation. These challenges can slow down market penetration, especially in lower-income or legacy infrastructure areas.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;"><strong>Retrofit Opportunities Amidst Growing Awareness</strong></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">With fire safety regulations becoming more stringent, there is a growing market for retrofitting fire protection systems in older structures. However, these upgrades often involve connecting fire detection and suppression systems to standpipe systems, modifying electrical wiring, and integrating alarm networks&mdash;all of which can lead to higher costs.</p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">Building retrofits aim to deliver maximum safety with minimal disruptions. Still, due to the complex nature and cost of such projects, many developers and facility managers delay implementation unless mandated by law. Overcoming these retrofit challenges remains a key market opportunity for system integrators and solution providers who can offer cost-efficient, non-invasive solutions.</p>
